# Enterprise buying center (collective purchase)

Enterprise purchases are made by buying groups; expert power is typically the strongest influence inside them. The unit of marketing is an evidence package per stakeholder, not one pitch.

| Stakeholder | Decides on | Give them |
|---|---|---|
| Technical champion | Workflow fit, integration effort, developer sentiment | Architecture/integration maps, real-workflow pilots, migration paths |
| Security | Exposure, controls, supply chain | Security posture docs, certifications, data-handling and provenance detail |
| Procurement/legal | Contract risk, lock-in, licensing | Open standards, exit paths, licensing clarity, SLAs |
| Finance | Cost logic, ROI plausibility | Cost model on their usage shape; pilot-measured outcomes, never borrowed benchmarks |
| Executive sponsor | Strategic fit, vendor viability | Roadmap credibility, same-industry references, ecosystem stability |

Do:
- Design pilots as de-risking instruments (minimize switching risk and legal exposure), not demos.
- Deploy credibility signals (analysts, standards bodies, peer firms) early, while the category is unfamiliar.
- Provide governance transparency — model cards, limitations, support structure. Builds institutional trust even without lifting immediate preference.

Don't:
- One champion-focused narrative — the deal dies on the ignored stakeholder.
- Overclaim anything that will surface in security review.
- Treat procurement friction as an obstacle instead of an audience.

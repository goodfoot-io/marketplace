# Situations

Scope: create entry points by the condition under which a reader needs information, not only by topic. Owns the situational-routing rules for Principle 1.

## Diagnostics → actions

- **The reader is calm and exploratory vs. under incident pressure**: choose explanatory depth or terse operational guidance accordingly — `reference/principles/02-typed-source-of-truth-topics/modes-and-genres.md`.
- **The reader is reading before, during, or after action**: sequence the page around preparation, execution, or recovery.
- **The reader arrives from memory, a ticket, an alert, a customer report, or a code path**: add aliases and entry links matching each origin — `reference/principles/04-multiple-paths/entry-points.md`, `reference/principles/05-controlled-vocabulary-and-metadata/vocabulary.md`.
- **The reader may not know the official term**: add synonyms, aliases, and "also called" labels — `reference/principles/05-controlled-vocabulary-and-metadata/vocabulary.md`.
- **The reader searches by symptom, error, component, or business concept**: title and index the page for all likely frames — `reference/principles/06-information-scent/titles.md`, `reference/principles/07-nonlinear-information-seeking/search-behavior.md`.
- **The reader needs a quick answer vs. a full explanation**: put the quick answer first and link to depth — `reference/principles/07-nonlinear-information-seeking/progressive-disclosure.md`.
- **The reader arrives directly from search or retrieval**: include enough local context for the page to work without the intended navigation — `reference/principles/07-nonlinear-information-seeking/index.md`.
- **The reader may act in the wrong environment**: surface environment and version markers before the instructions — `reference/principles/05-controlled-vocabulary-and-metadata/metadata-fields.md`.

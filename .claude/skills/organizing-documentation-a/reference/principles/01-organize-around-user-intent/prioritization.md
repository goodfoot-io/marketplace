# Prioritization

Scope: rank which intents to serve first by frequency, value, and cost of failure, and turn unmet intents into work. Owns the prioritization rules for Principle 1.

## Diagnostics → actions

- **An intent is most common**: give it the shortest, clearest path — `reference/principles/06-information-scent/cost-of-click.md`.
- **An intent is most valuable to support**: elevate it even when it is not the most frequent.
- **An intent is most costly when unsupported**: treat it as a high-priority gap — `reference/principles/08-validate-and-iterate/support-and-ops.md`.
- **An intent produces repeated support interruptions**: convert the repeated answer into a source-of-truth page or FAQ with a clear scope — `reference/principles/08-validate-and-iterate/support-and-ops.md`.
- **An intent is blocked by missing documentation**: create the source-of-truth topic or a backlog item after confirming the gap — `reference/principles/02-typed-source-of-truth-topics/topic-identity.md`.
- **An intent is blocked by confusing documentation**: rewrite labels, split overloaded pages, or improve routing — `reference/principles/06-information-scent/index.md`, `reference/principles/02-typed-source-of-truth-topics/canonicality.md`.
- **An intent is blocked because docs are organized by team instead of user need**: re-map pages into user-task and lifecycle projections — `reference/principles/04-multiple-paths/facets.md`.
- **An intent is low-frequency but compliance-, incident-, or safety-critical**: promote it despite low volume and review it more rigorously — `reference/principles/08-validate-and-iterate/governance.md`.
- **An intent is cited from tickets, alerts, or code**: give its page a durable identifier and preserve access through links when paths change — `reference/foundations.md` stable-identifier section.

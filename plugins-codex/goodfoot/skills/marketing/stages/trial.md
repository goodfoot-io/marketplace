# Trial

Mechanism: trial is won by low setup cost, reversibility, and an immediate win on the user's own material. Defaults and pre-configuration genuinely drive first use — but they buy trial, not value.

Do:
- Collapse time-to-first-success on the reader's own project and headline it ("first result in your repo in five minutes").
- State reversibility and sandboxing explicitly — perceived risk of trying is a bigger barrier than enthusiasm is a driver ("local, undoable, gone in one command").
- Time CTAs at workflow boundaries (new project, new sprint, new hire), never mid-task.
- Ship good defaults; make the sanctioned path the easy path.

Don't:
- Demand configuration before showing value.
- Demo-data first-runs that postpone the "works on MY stuff" moment.
- Dark-pattern defaults — inflated enrollment past what the product confirms poisons retention.

Example:
- ✗ "Book a demo to see how Acme transforms your workflow."
- ✓ "`brew install acme && acme init` — first finding on your repo in five minutes. Local, nothing leaves your machine, `acme purge` removes every trace."
